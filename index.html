Experiments around the hall

  1. Throwing lane: pick up nearby balls with E; hold Q and release to throw toward the center of the camera. E drops without a throwing impulse. Different masses and sizes produce different drag and recoil.
  2. Drop tower: release equal-size 8 kg and 0.25 kg balls from the same 8 m clearance. In vacuum they fall together. Compare impact times on the tower display. At high air density the light sphere can float upward.
  3. Grip lanes: compare concrete, high-grip mat and low-friction polymer. Low gravity reduces normal force and traction; high pressure changes drag, not the friction coefficient.
  4. Mobility course: step platforms and gap platforms use solid collision surfaces. Jump onto them. The powered lift rises level with the 4 m balcony and transfers contact motion; it is an external energy source. The Water & wings menu can also place you on the balcony directly.
  5. Pendulum: walk into the heavy suspended sphere, or throw a ball at it. Its fixed-length, tension-only tether and air drag respond to chamber conditions.
  6. Bicycle: approach and press F. W supplies 250 W pedal power; Shift supplies an assumed 450 W effort while held. S brakes, A/D steer. Tire force requires ground contact and is grip-limited. A virtual balance assist keeps it upright.

Gravity, gas and buoyancy

Gravity is uniform downward, 0–98.0665 m/s². Chamber floor pressure is absolute, 0–10.1325 MPa. Dry air remains at 293.15 K. Density and viscosity are interpolated from an offline table generated with CoolProp's Lemmon dry-air equation of state, including real-gas behavior. Local pressure follows hydrostatic balance dp/dy = −ρg through the hall. Vacuum produces zero aerodynamic drag and buoyancy. Uniform ambient pressure does not produce a separate net force; displaced-gas buoyancy is ρVg.

Forces and contacts

Motion advances at a fixed 240 Hz. Gravity, buoyancy, drag, muscular forces, contact impulses and object recoil change velocity. Drag uses relative speed in still chamber air; spheres use a low-Reynolds-number correction and assumed rough-sphere Cd = 0.47 at higher Reynolds numbers. Drag integration cannot reverse a velocity. Sphere collisions transfer linear and angular momentum with dissipative friction and restitution. Rigid bodies retain their assumed volume at every pressure.

A limited-strength human

The character is a 100 kg equipped person with 0.10 m³ displaced volume. Assumed sustained support capacity is 3,000 N and peak leg force is 3,600 N. Walking targets 1.4 m/s; Shift targets a steady 3.0 m/s run. Gravity changes cadence and contact time; above moderate loading the target pace gradually decreases. Earth running uses short alternating flights; lower gravity extends the airborne stride, while higher gravity requires more frequent contacts. Feet plant during stance, arms counter-swing, and the pelvis anticipates landing. Friction, finite muscle force (up to 520 N horizontally), power (650 W horizontally) and drag can limit the achieved speed. The display shows horizontal speed and planned cadence. Jump energy is limited by leg work and weight. As support demand approaches the strength limit, movement weakens and the character crouches; sufficiently high load prevents locomotion. These thresholds are illustrative, not individual human tolerance predictions. Arms can exert up to 220 N while holding an object. Throws transfer an equal opposite impulse to the character, visible in zero g. Free-air WASD supplies no direct thrust; equipped wings require air, and swimming requires liquid.

In zero g, WASD selects a direction and the model pushes from a touching or reachable surface when possible. Space also requests a push; friction makes a sideways floor push include an upward component. Without surface contact, the limbs settle into a relaxed floating pose: there is no walking or running cycle. Coast or throw an object for recoil. Recover person relocates the character explicitly and is a test reset, not a physical force. Internal pose changes and viewing direction are procedural; full articulated angular momentum, muscles, injury and balance failure are not solved.

New mobility and fluid experiments

Caps Lock toggles fast effort; Shift still works while held. WASD + Space makes a directional jump using one shared work budget. High load progresses to prone crawling with finite pull and belly friction. Water & wings opens three arm-wing sizes, the swimming basin, phase readings and a thermal forecast. Wings require aerodynamic force and finite arm power; water uses 1,196 representative fluid parcels and a separate mass/enthalpy balance. The body poses blend between these modes.

Display performance

Nearby equipment and water retain detail; distant objects, surface ripples and spray use simpler rendering. Display resolution adapts to workload. Physics timing and forces stay unchanged.

Interpretation limits

The hall, suit, lightweight test shells and solid bike tires are assumed rigid and pressure-resistant. Water boiling, evaporative cooling, freezing and sublimation use a bulk enthalpy model. Full vapor flow, gas thermal transients, breathing, oxygen toxicity, crushing, suit engineering and human survival are outside the model. Environment changes are controlled resets of the ambient field; they can alter potential energy and are not closed-system experiments. No air-current swimming, acoustic model, deformation, detailed suspension or aerodynamic wakes are included. The contact and gait models are reduced-order approximations, not a validated biomechanics or safety simulator. At very low nonzero pressures the continuum drag approximation becomes less accurate.

Flight is an experiment

On the ground, WASD moves the legs. Airborne, WASD directs wing strokes for forward, backward or sideways flight; Space requests upward strokes and C downward strokes. Shift or Caps Lock requests greater effort. Space jumps with the held direction, or requests upward effort in flight. Wing lift, stall, drag and arm power are limited. Braced spars and a torso harness support passive gliding loads (0.9 / 2.2 / 4.5 kN for the three sizes); heavy loading locks out flapping. The assumed brace automatically feathers at its load limit. Dense air can support a high-gravity glide with sufficient speed and area, but does not guarantee takeoff. Earth conditions usually will not support human-powered arm-wing flight. Try 0.05 g / 10 atm with large wings, then compare conditions.

Waterfall & ceiling rain

Two marked stations are at the north end of the hall. Approach a button and press G, or click the nearby Start/Stop button. The waterfall recirculates pool water through a 6 m outlet. The ceiling nozzles spray downward over the drained rain bay. Pumps have finite flow and power: higher gravity can reduce flow. Switching off leaves airborne water moving. In zero g the pumps still eject water; gravity no longer makes it fall.

Water carries mass, momentum and heat. Air density changes drag; low pressure can cool and freeze exposed droplets. Collectors return intercepted water through plumbing, so no puddles accumulate on the drained floor. Fine spray, sheet breakup, humidity and turbulent air motion are approximations.

Pool / north-east corner

An 8 × 10 m basin, 2.4 m deep, with submerged exit steps along its east edge. WASD swims; Space rises; C dives. Move toward a nearby edge to tread water and pull out; submerged steps transition to wading. Hold C to stay underwater instead. Water density responds weakly to pressure. The liquid uses 1,196 moving fluid parcels with density constraints, drag, buoyancy and wake interaction. At zero g, view the individual liquid parcels; a flat surface is no longer assumed.

Boiling, cooling and freezing

Lower pressure below the water's saturation pressure to boil it. Escaping vapor removes mass and heat; the water cools. At sufficiently low pressure it can freeze, then lose ice by sublimation. Chamber pressure is actively maintained by an assumed external exhaust, and the basin receives heat from 20°C surroundings. Reset pool fill restores 20°C water.

White bubble markers indicate boiling; bubble shapes and vapor flow are not resolved. The thermal model treats the pool as well mixed. The rate coefficient and wall heat transfer are assumptions, so the times below are estimates.
